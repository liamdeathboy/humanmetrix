How we work

Three commitments that shape every model on this site

Public data only

Every model is built from public-use files — CDC BRFSS, NHANES, the American Community Survey. Nothing proprietary, nothing behind a paywall, nothing you cannot download and check yourself.

Methods in the open

The fitting script ships alongside the model. Coefficients are published as plain JSON. If you disagree with a specification, you have everything you need to refit it and show us we were wrong.

Uncertainty stated first

Each model reports how much better it does than simply guessing the national average. Often the answer is: not much. That belongs on the page, not buried in a footnote.

Method

What these models can and cannot tell you

They describe groups, not people. A model that knows your age, income and state knows almost nothing about you specifically. It knows what the average of everyone who answered identically looks like. Those are very different claims, and the second one is the only one we make.

Survey weights are applied throughout. Federal surveys oversample some groups and undersample others by design. Every figure here is weighted back to the national population, which is why our numbers match published CDC estimates rather than the raw file.

Correlation is all a cross-section can give you. These surveys photograph a population once. When exercise and body weight move together, nothing in the data says which came first, and we do not pretend otherwise.

Self-reported data carries self-reported error. People shave weight and add height. Where a measured comparison exists, we show it rather than quietly hoping you do not notice.

About

An independent project, not an institution

Human Metrix is an independent effort to make federal survey data legible to people who will never open a SAS transport file. No funding, no affiliation with any agency whose data appears here, and no interest in telling you what to conclude.
